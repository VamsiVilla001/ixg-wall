Wall behaviour

Layout

Every tile keeps one size, so feeds and stats stay legible however many there are; scroll the wall for the rest. Only feeds on screen stream at this quality. One scrolled away keeps playing and stays monitored, and drops back to 480p after a minute. If the laptop or link can't keep up, the quality is held back as for priority feeds.

Every feed stays on screen, so tiles shrink as you add feeds; 20–30 feeds stream at 480p.

YouTube API

Adds live viewers, likes, views and comments to every feed, and a viewer history with peak, average, lowest and trend: open a feed's Stats for the full analytics. Also privacy, embedding, HD/SD and live chat, at no extra quota. The backend polls YouTube once per interval for every open window: every 30 s uses about 3,000 of the daily 10,000 quota units for up to 50 feeds.

Get a key: in Google Cloud, enable YouTube Data API v3, then create an API key under Credentials. Restrict it to the YouTube Data API; an address restriction must allow http://localhost:8080/*. The key is saved with the wall on this laptop (%LOCALAPPDATA%\IXG Wall), never in the project folder.

Laptop & rendering

Measured

Applies to the managed wall window and restarts it. On the benchmark laptop (Ryzen 5 4600H, Radeon graphics) software decoding dropped 0% of frames at 30 feeds; the GPU decoder dropped 4–7% at 24.

Drift is measured against the lowest lag that feed reached since it loaded. Three jumps inside two minutes escalate to a full refresh. Anything over 90 s behind jumps regardless.

Destroys and recreates the player, releasing its video buffer and memory. Refreshes are staggered so the wall never blanks at once. 0 turns this off.

Bandwidth & loading

Your link's download speed from a speed test. Priority feeds are only boosted while the wall's estimated load stays under 80% of it. 0 means unknown: boosts are granted and shed one at a time when several feeds stall together.

Feeds load in order, priority first, so startup and refreshes never hit the link all at once. Standard feeds stream at YouTube's embed floor of 480p; YouTube ignores requests to go lower.
