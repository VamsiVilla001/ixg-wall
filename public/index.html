          Wall behaviour

          Layout

          Every tile keeps one size, so feeds and stats stay legible however many there are; scroll the wall for the rest. Only feeds on screen stream at this quality. One scrolled away keeps playing and stays monitored, and drops back to 480p after a minute. If the laptop or link can't keep up, the quality is held back as for priority feeds.

          Every feed stays on screen, so tiles shrink as you add feeds; 20–30 feeds stream at 480p.

          YouTube API

          Adds live viewers, likes, views and comments to every feed, and a viewer history with peak, average, lowest and trend: open a feed's Stats for the full analytics. Also privacy, embedding, HD/SD and live chat, at no extra quota. The backend polls YouTube once per interval for every open window: every 30 s uses about 3,000 of the daily 10,000 quota units for up to 50 feeds.

          Get a key: in Google Cloud, enable YouTube Data API v3, then create an API key under Credentials. Restrict it to the YouTube Data API. The key stays on the server (secrets.json in its data folder) and is never sent back to a browser. For an address restriction, allow the server's public IP on a hosted wall, or http://localhost:8080/* on the laptop.

          Channel sign-in · ingest health

          Sign in with the Google account that owns the channel the feeds stream to (read-only). The wall then shows how each feed's encoder stream arrives at YouTube: health (good, ok, bad), resolution, frame rate and YouTube's warnings, refreshed with the numbers above.

          Laptop & rendering

          Measured

          Applies to the managed wall window and restarts it. On the benchmark laptop (Ryzen 5 4600H, Radeon graphics) software decoding dropped 0% of frames at 30 feeds; the GPU decoder dropped 4–7% at 24.

          A website can't choose how the browser decodes video. On a low-spec laptop that drops frames, open the wall from a Chrome shortcut that adds --disable-accelerated-video-decode (software decoding: 0% dropped frames at 30 feeds on the benchmark laptop, against 4–7% on its GPU). DEPLOY.md has the exact shortcut.

          Sync

          Every feed carries the same input, so the wall holds all live feeds at one shared delay, read from YouTube's timestamp on each frame. The delay is the slowest feed's edge delay plus this margin. Feeds that are ahead are held back with YouTube's live rewind; feeds that fall behind catch up. Offsets up to 10 s close smoothly at 0.75× or 1.25×; bigger ones jump (YouTube lands jumps on segment boundaries, so speed finishes the job). A feed whose edge delay is more than 15 s past the others isn't synced, so it can't hold up the whole wall.

          Releasing build-up on this PC

          For feeds outside the sync group. Behind edge is measured from the least delay that feed has reached since it loaded. Up to 15 s closes at 1.25×; more jumps. Three jumps inside two minutes escalate to a full refresh, and anything over 90 s behind jumps regardless.

          Destroys and recreates the player, releasing everything it holds in this PC's memory. Refreshes are staggered so the wall never blanks at once. 0 turns this off.

          What the numbers mean

          A frame travels: encoder → YouTube → network → this PC's player → screen. Each reading belongs to one of those steps.

          Encoder Your side
          Encodes the picture and sends it to YouTube in order. Its own buffer and dropped frames show in the encoder's telemetry. The wall shows how its stream arrives: Ingest health (channel sign-in).
          Edge delay YouTube
          The least delay a feed can have: YouTube receiving, processing and packaging the video, mostly set by the broadcast's latency mode (Normal / Low / Ultra-low in YouTube Studio). The wall can't release it, only report it.
          Connection Network
          How fast YouTube's video reaches this PC against the feed's bitrate. Below 1.2× the bitrate, rebuffering is coming (Feed Meter extension).
          Buffer ahead This PC
          Video already downloaded and waiting to play. A cushion, not delay: red below 4 s.
          Rebuffering This PC
          The player ran out of buffer and stopped. Every second stopped adds to Behind edge.
          Behind edge This PC
          How far the player sits behind the newest video YouTube has. This is the build-up the wall releases: catching up, jumping or refreshing.
          Delay Whole chain
          From YouTube's timestamp on a frame to this screen: Edge delay + Behind edge. It uses this PC's clock, so keep Windows time synced; any clock offset is the same for every feed and doesn't affect sync.
          Sync Wall
          Each feed's offset from the wall's shared delay. + has more delay than the others, − less. Green within ±0.3 s.

          Bandwidth & loading

          Your link's download speed from a speed test. Priority feeds are only boosted while the wall's estimated load stays under 80% of it. 0 means unknown: boosts are granted and shed one at a time when several feeds stall together.

          Feeds load in order, priority first, so startup and refreshes never hit the link all at once. Standard feeds stream at YouTube's embed floor of 480p; YouTube ignores requests to go lower.
